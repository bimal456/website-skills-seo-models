# Semantic SEO, Avalanche sequencing and cannibalization

## Evidence and scope
Treat semantic planning as an editorial workflow. Treat CCarter's Avalanche methodology as a prioritization hypothesis, not Google's documented traffic-tier system or a guarantee of rankings. Do not claim algorithm immunity or guaranteed traffic. Research current SERPs and primary facts before publishing. Preserve the brief's explicit word counts, headings and brand/affiliate distinctions.

## Semantic content map
Before writing, create seo-map.json with one row per planned indexable page: path, primary_keyword, cluster_id, intent, geo, language, primary_entity, unique_value, parent_path, secondary_keywords, entities, required_questions, sources and publish_wave. Define actual entity attributes and relationships, such as brand -> bonus -> wagering basis -> eligible games -> cashout limits, or payment method -> deposit -> withdrawal -> verification. Cover the user's task and relevant constraints with supported facts, not entity name lists or mechanically inserted synonyms.
Choose a hub and supporting pages by distinct intent. Assign one primary URL per intent cluster within a GEO/language. Put synonyms and close variants on that URL when the SERPs and tasks align. Split pages only for meaningfully different needs, supported by SERP evidence and a unique-value statement. Do not force a page per keyword, question, payment variant or location. Preserve exact supplied headings while flagging contradictions for resolution.
For brand sites, focus the map on the owned brand's real products, games, payments, account help and policies. For affiliate sites, distinguish comparison hubs, third-party reviews, offer-specific analysis and educational content; no official operator voice. Record dated primary sources for commercial facts. Provide direct answers, explanatory context and useful comparisons without unsupported semantic scores or invented ranking formulas.

## Avalanche-inspired publication waves
Record available Search Console clicks/impressions, existing rankings and relevant conversions for a defined period, GEO and branded/nonbranded scope. If absent, label the site a new-site baseline; never invent a tier. Use Ahrefs or another available source for dated keyword estimates, then inspect actual SERP competition, intent, domain fit and information gaps. Ahrefs access is separate from model access; never claim it was used without evidence.
Prioritize achievable, useful lower-competition clusters first, then broader competitive hubs when observed performance supports expansion. Keep required navigation and conversion pages even when they are competitive. Do not delay the homepage or force daily publishing just to match the method. Set proposed waves and review them after a meaningful measurement period; lack of traffic alone does not prove a tier ceiling.
Use KGR only as an optional recorded heuristic: allintitle result estimate / monthly search estimate, using the same keyword, GEO and date. Do not compute with zero or unknown volume; label unavailable. Search-result counts are approximate and volatile. Do not use KGR or keyword difficulty alone to approve a page. Do not reproduce traffic-tier arithmetic as a forecast.

## Cannibalization prevention and diagnosis
Create one query-to-URL owner map. Normalize keyword spelling and URLs for comparison; manually inspect equivalent synonyms, same-task outlines and substantial content overlap. Run scripts/qa_seo_map.py --map seo-map.json to catch duplicate paths, competing cluster owners and repeated keyword/intent targets. The checker cannot infer semantic equivalence or prove live cannibalization.
Before creating a page, compare it with existing URLs using current sitemap, crawl and content. Distinct review, bonus and login intents may coexist when useful; repeated keywords alone are not cannibalization. Multiple URLs receiving impressions alone are also insufficient evidence. Where data exists, inspect query/page performance by country, device and date, URL switching, intended owner, conversions and intent match. Export a conflict report with evidence and proposed decisions; label live diagnosis not tested on a new site.
Resolve genuine same-intent conflicts by choosing the strongest useful owner, consolidating unique content and updating internal links. On authorized existing-site changes, use a 301 only for a permanently superseded page; verify it, remove redirect chains and update sitemaps. Use canonicals for truly duplicate or near-duplicate alternatives, not as a substitute for merging different pages. Do not automatically noindex, delete or redirect based on similarity scores. Preserve valuable distinct intents. Make indexable sitemap URLs, canonicals and internal links agree on the intended owner.

## Internal links, GEO and final QA
Link hub to relevant supporting pages and back where useful; add contextual lateral links only when they help the reader. Use descriptive varied anchors tied to the destination's actual topic. Avoid sitewide exact-match repetition and artificial all-to-all linking. Check orphan pages, navigation reachability, relevant breadcrumbs, link destinations and migration chains.
For GEO variants, verify real differences in eligibility, currency, payments, offers and regulation. Translation or swapping country names alone is insufficient. Use hreflang only for genuine equivalents, with reciprocal valid references. Do not canonicalize distinct localized pages to another GEO merely to hide overlap.
Release only after the owner map and conflicts are reviewed. Deliver seo-map.json, a dated sources/claims ledger, publication waves, internal-link plan and cannibalization report with resolved, unresolved and not-tested items. Semantic coverage and live ranking checks require evidence beyond the script. Do not guarantee zero future cannibalization.
